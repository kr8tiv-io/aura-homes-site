The shell is the room before it becomes a particular life. A long, low box on a steel frame. Polycarbonate in place of heavy glass: clear, mirror, or tint, smooth rather than ribbed. It rests in the meadow instead of arguing with it.

Morning is the honest test. Light comes in low, across the grass, and the mirror band gives some of it back to the field. You make coffee. The room is already near the temperature of the day, close enough that the door wants to be open.

This is for land you already have. A corner past the house. An edge where the mowing stops. The shell does not replace the house. It gives that edge a floor, a roof, and a place to sit when the weather is worth meeting.

Steel keeps the line thin. The polycarbonate keeps the walls light. What you live with is the meadow, framed, and the quiet of a room that does not pretend to be larger than it is.
